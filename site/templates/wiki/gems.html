How Gems Work in Trove

Gems are the single biggest source of power on a Trove character - and the most confusing. This is the whole system, taken apart piece by piece and made interactive. Scroll to build one up; poke at everything.

  1. 1 — Its tier sets the ceiling

    A gem's tier sets its ceiling - how big its stats get and how far it can level. Trove has more tiers than these, but the four worth building around are Radiant, Stellar, Crystal and Mystic. Leveling never changes a gem's tier - but a Converter can raise it a tier while keeping its progress.

  2. 2 — Lesser or Empowered

    A Lesser gem is locked to one damage school. An Empowered gem drops that restriction, rolls stronger, and carries a special ability. You slot far fewer Empowered gems, so they matter more.

  3. 3 — An element gives it a home

    Each gem is Water, Fire, Air or Cosmic, and only slots into the matching gem socket. Cosmic is the odd one out - it always carries Light.

  4. 4 — Its stats, rolled

    The gem rolls with two or three stats from a pool - damage, crit and health - each at a random strength. Three-stat gems are the only keepers; a two-stat gem can never catch up, so check the count before the rolls.

  5. 5 — Level it, then perfect it

    From there you feed it Gem Dust to level up - climbing Power Rank and unlocking extra rolls - then spend focuses to push every stat to 100%. That's the whole loop.

02

The tiers that matter

Gems come in more tiers than these, but only Radiant, Stellar, Crystal and Mystic hold stats worth building around. Each tier is a higher ceiling than the last, so late-game power lives almost entirely in Crystal and Mystic gems.

Converters — move a gem up a tier

Leveling never changes a gem's tier, but two Store items do - and they carry the gem's level and every augment straight across, so a maxed gem never starts over.

You never need a converter - higher-tier gems drop on their own. It's just a shortcut to carry a gem you've already poured levels and focuses into up a tier, instead of starting over on a fresh drop.

03

Lesser vs Empowered

Two kinds of gem. Lesser gems are restricted to one damage school; Empowered gems drop the restriction, hit harder, and carry a special ability.

Where Lesser gems come from

You don't craft Lesser gems - you find them. Each tier starts dropping at its own world difficulty, so the gems you pick up get better as the worlds you can clear do.

Delves are the shortcut to Mystic

Delves parallel the same difficulties, and the deeper you go the higher they climb - depth 160+ is the D15 equivalent and drops the same Mystic gem boxes. What it doesn't ask for is a D15 character:

Delve, depth 160+ 15,000 Power Rank No Light requirement
D15 world 42,500 Power Rank and 10,000 Light

Same gems, a third of the power. If friends can carry you that deep, you can skip Stellar and Crystal entirely and start collecting Mystic gems long before you could set foot in a D15 world.

04

Class Gems

Every class has one gem all its own. A Class Gem is a special Empowered gem, locked to a single class, that rewrites one of its abilities - a build-defining upgrade you slot like any other Empowered gem.

One per class

Each class has its own Class Gem, and only that class can equip it. Switch class and you swap to a different one.

Rewrites an ability

Rather than just stats, it upgrades one of the class's abilities - like Dino Tamer's Hunting Dart firing three darts, or Pirate Captain's First Mate cannons hitting twice as hard.

How you get one: take a Class Gem Key into the Shadow Colosseum in the Shores of the Everdark, clear it, and open the Everdark Vault that appears - it drops the Class Gem for whichever class you're playing at the time.

05

Gem elements

Element sets a gem's slot on your character and colors what it can roll. Water, Fire and Air behave the same way; Cosmic plays by its own rules.

06

Two or three stats

A gem rolls with two or three stats, each pulled from a pool at a random strength. Only three-stat gems are worth keeping - a two-stat gem can never catch up - and the rolls are what focusing later fixes.

The stat pool

A gem draws its stats from a fixed pool decided by its type and element:

    Damage, Critical Damage, Critical Hit and the two Max Health stats make up the pool. Cosmic gems lock one slot to Light.

    The one rule that decides a keeper

    How many stats a gem rolls is locked at level 1 - no dust or focus ever adds a missing one, so the stat count is the first thing to check. In practice only Empowered gems roll a random count - every Lesser gem from Stellar up already has all three.

    Keeper

    3 stats at level 1 - the only gems worth leveling. It then picks up a boost at 5, 10 and 15, ending at 6 containers.

    Trash

    2 stats or fewer - automatic scrap. It can never catch a 3-stat gem, so don't spend a single dust on it.

    Lvl 13 stats + Lvl 5boost + Lvl 10boost + Lvl 15boost = 6containers
    07

    Leveling & Power Rank

    Feeding a gem Gem Dust raises its level, which grows every stat and adds to your Power Rank. Milestone levels give the biggest jumps - and unlock extra rolls on the gem.

    Level 1 / 23
    Power Rank 0 perfect Mystic Empowered gem

    Boosters — better odds each level

    Every level-up is a roll: it can raise the gem one level, or double level for two at once. Gems no longer crack, so a bad roll only costs the materials, never progress. Boosters are one-shot items you spend on the attempt to tilt the odds - the stronger the booster, the more level-ups land and the more of them double, so the gem maxes out in far fewer tries.

    Ninth & Tenth Life share the same numbers but never overlap - Ninth Life covers every tier except Mystic, and Mystic gems take only Tenth Life in its place.

    Upcycling: farming Gem Dust

    Loot-collect a gem and it returns Gem Dust based on its level - and a Crystal gem leveled to 25 gives back more dust than it took to get there. Leveling Crystal gems purely to loot-collect them for that surplus is called upcycling. A Gem Fixture trims the upgrade cost by 20%, or a Gem Fixture plus a Gem Day from Patron by 40% - both grow the net return:

    Gem Fixture · −20% +4,045,666 net dust per 50 gems
    + Gem Day · −40% +4,854,799 net dust per 50 gems

    Never level with boosters when you're farming dust. A booster costs more than the extra dust it hands back, so it only eats into the profit - level the plain way.

    Level every Crystal gem you get to 25 - even ones you'll never equip - then loot-collect them for the dust. End-game gems burn through a lot of it, and this is the steadiest way to bank it.

    08

    Perfecting with focuses

    A stat almost never rolls at 100%. Focus items push it up the rest of the way - three grades, each worth a fixed slice of quality. The trick is spending the fewest, most efficient focuses to hit the cap.

    71% quality

    Apply focuses to close the gap to a perfect stat:

    Focuses only add quality up to 100%; overshooting is wasted. The Gem Evaluator plans the exact, cheapest set of focuses for a real gem.

    Rerolled wrong? Reshape it with Chaos

    Focus fixes a stat's quality - but not which stats you rolled or where the boosts landed. Two items fix the layout itself:

    Chaos Spark

    Rerolls one stat's type - swap a stat you don't want (say Max Health) for one you do (Critical Hit). It picks a new stat at random from whatever's still valid.

    Can't reroll a locked Light stat, duplicate a stat you already have, or give you both Physical and Magic damage.

    Chaos Flare

    Moves one boost container from one stat onto another. If a milestone boost landed on a throwaway stat, flare relocates that container to a stat that matters.

    Only works on a stat holding more than one container (one that caught a boost); the container lands on a random other stat.

    What Sparks and Flares really cost

    Both are crafted, and both burn Lunar Souls and Bound Brilliance. Neither drops from ordinary farming - each week hands you a fixed allowance and playing more won't raise it, so reshaping a gem is paced by the calendar rather than by effort.

    Bound Brilliance isn't only a Chaos ingredient - every focus you spend costs one too. Between that and a capped supply of Souls, a perfect gem is measured in weeks rather than hours, so put both into the gem you actually mean to finish.

    Advanced Under the hood: how a gem really stores its stats

    In-game a stat shows a single number, but underneath it's a stack of hidden containers - and that stack is what really decides how hard a gem is to perfect. Every stat starts with one container; each milestone boost (level 5, 10, 15) drops one more onto a random stat, so a maxed gem holds six containers spread across its three stats.

    Magic Dmg · 3
    Crit Dmg · 2
    Light · 1
    base container boost container (from a milestone) empty top = what focus has to fill

    Containers overflow

    Each container has its own random base roll (0–100%), fixed at creation - that's the RNG. Focus stacks on top (Rough +2.5%, Precise +5%, Superior +12.5% each). A stat's quality is the average of its containers, and a container can overflow past 100% to carry a low one - so 112.5% + 87.5% averages to a perfect 100% stat. The stat caps at 100%, so overflow beyond that adds no power; it just flattens out.

    Why boosts cost you

    Every boost is another container you must fill to reach a perfect gem. More boosts means more power but a pricier cap - and because they land at random, one stat can end up with several low-rolled containers that each eat a pile of focus. "How hard to perfect" = how many containers × how low they rolled. That's the exact sum the Evaluator solves.

    09

    Gem Builds

    Once your gems are perfect, a "build" is how you spread their boosts across stats to squeeze out the last drop of damage. It's deep end-game min-maxing that most players never need - but here's exactly how a build code reads. Hover or tap any number.

    What the "+" hides

    A plain four-number code like 9/0 3/15 leaves the Cosmic gems out - in a damage build they always sit at + 0/0/3 0/0/6 (every Cosmic boost poured into Light), so printing them would just be clutter.

    What a Farm build changes

    A Farm build pulls Cosmic boosts off Light and onto Damage - you give up some Light to hit harder. That's the only time the + segment shows real numbers, like 3/0/0 1/1/4.

    Bottom line: build codes wring tiny, hard-won gains out of an already-maxed set. Skip them unless you're chasing the very top of a leaderboard - and when you are, let the Gem Builds optimizer find the best layout for your class.
